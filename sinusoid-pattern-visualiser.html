Sinusoid Field Σ sin(k·r + φ)

Three carriers A, B, C form the columns of matrix M. Rows 1–2 of each column are the cartesian wave numbers (x, y) set by dragging a dot; row 3 is its phase φ, set by rotating the dot's direction needle through 0 → 2π. The wave value sweeps through an editable gradient of your colours, and distortion nodes can be dropped on the canvas to warp the field. GPU-rendered at up to 1920 × 1080, exporting a smooth 60 fps looping WebM for OBS — or a GIF.

Canvas

Drag a dot's body to move it (x, y wave numbers). Drag the needle tip to rotate its phase. Edge sharpness narrows every colour transition — at 0 the original soft gradient, at 1 crisp defined bands.

Distortion nodes — drag onto the canvas

Drop a node on the preview to warp the field where it lands (up to 6 at once). Drag a placed node's centre to move it, drag the small handle on its dashed ring to grow or shrink its area of influence, and double-click a node to remove it. The Mirror node is a line instead of a circle: it reflects the whole field across itself — drag its centre to reposition the line and drag its orange handle to change the angle. The snap toggles above constrain the mirror line to a grid and/or 5° angle steps; leave both off for completely free placement. All distortions animate with the loop, so exports stay seamless.

Colours — gradient stops

The bar is the full 0–100% range of the wave value. Drag a node left or right to change that colour's position and share of the blend — drag one past another to reorder the colours. Adding a colour drops a new node into the widest gap. Pick colours in the swatch row, which always mirrors left-to-right bar order; hover a swatch and hit × to remove it (minimum two).

Matrix M — columns A B C

value = Σᵢ (1−ρ)·sin(2π(xᵢfx + yᵢfy) + φᵢ + t) + ρ·sin(2π‖(xᵢ,yᵢ)‖·rᵢ + φᵢ + t), evaluated on distortion-warped coordinates, normalised, then mapped across the gradient stops at their node positions with per-segment smoothstep width set by sharpness.

Export

The WebM always captures exactly one full period of the sinusoidal interference at 60 fps — the point where every carrier's phase returns to its start, so the first and last frames are identical and it loops seamlessly. The clip's length in seconds follows from Loop seconds above (how long one period takes). In OBS add it as a Media Source and tick Loop.
